# Working principle

## Solar heating (greenhouse effect)
Short-wave solar radiation passes through the transparent dome and is absorbed by the produce and the galvanised trays. They re-emit long-wave heat, which the dome largely blocks, and the dome also stops the warm air from blowing away. The air inside therefore becomes warmer, and its relative humidity falls.

## Moisture removal
Warm air with low relative humidity takes water vapour from the surface of the produce. For continuous drying the moist air must leave the dome and be replaced by drier air. A small fan in the Ø250 mm top opening of the dome moves the moist air out; the airflow will be measured during testing.

## Rotation
A geared DC motor under the tabletop drives a sprocket on the vertical central shaft. The shaft passes through the centre hole of the tabletop and turns the cross-arm rack. Swivel casters at the ends of the arms roll on the tabletop and carry the weight of the rack, trays and produce, so the shaft and bearing only provide the turning torque and centring. Each tray passes in turn through the sunniest part of the dome, which aims to even out drying across trays.

## Key quantity
Moisture content, wet basis:

$$M_{wb} = \frac{W_i - W_d}{W_i} \times 100\%$$

where $W_i$ is the initial sample mass and $W_d$ the mass after drying.

Drying rate between two weighings:

$$DR = \frac{W_t - W_{t+\Delta t}}{\Delta t}$$
