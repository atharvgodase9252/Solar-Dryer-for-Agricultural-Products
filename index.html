Rotary Solar Dryer — for Agricultural Products

Farmers lose a large share of their fruit, vegetables, grain and spices after harvest because open-sun drying is slow, uneven and exposed to dust, insects, birds and rain. Our dryer puts the produce on multi-tier galvanised trays inside a transparent dome, and a geared motor slowly turns the whole tray rack so every tray gets the same sunlight. The prototype is built; drying tests are next, so no performance results are claimed yet.

SEC 02

The problem we are solving

Drying is the cheapest way to preserve farm produce, but most small farmers still dry it on the ground or on rooftops in open sun.

Open-sun drying is free, but the product is exposed to dust, insects, birds, animals and sudden rain; drying is slow and uneven, and quality and market value fall. Electric or fuel dryers solve this but cost too much to buy and run for a small farm. Simple box-type solar dryers are cheaper, yet trays near the front or the sunny side dry faster than trays at the back, so the farmer has to keep shifting trays by hand.

Our idea: a covered solar dryer whose tray rack rotates. A transparent dome traps solar heat and keeps the produce clean, and slow rotation gives every tray an equal share of sunlight — with no manual tray shuffling.

Drying approaches compared
ApproachPotential advantagesLimitations
Open-sun dryingNo equipment cost; any amount of produce.Dust, insects, birds and rain contamination; slow and uneven; weather-dependent; large losses.
Electric / fuel dryersFast, controlled temperature, works at night.High purchase and running cost; needs grid power or fuel.
Fixed box-type solar dryerLow cost; protected from contamination; faster than open sun.Uneven sunlight across trays; trays must be rotated by hand.
Our rotary solar dryerDome traps heat and protects produce; rotating rack aims at uniform exposure on every tray; low-cost materials (MS square tube, GI sheet, acrylic).Design aims, not results yet: drying rate, temperature rise and energy use are still to be measured.

The question we are answering

Can a low-cost, dome-covered solar dryer with a slowly rotating multi-tier tray rack dry agricultural produce faster and more evenly than open-sun drying, while keeping it clean?

SEC 03

How it is meant to work

01

Trap the sun

Sunlight passes through the transparent dome and is absorbed by the produce and the galvanised trays. The dome holds the warm air in (greenhouse effect), so the air inside is hotter and drier than outside.

02

Remove the moisture

Warm air picks up moisture from the produce. Moist air has to leave the dome and be replaced by drier air; ventilation openings and airflow are part of the testing plan.

03

Rotate for even drying

A geared motor under the table turns the central shaft through a chain and sprockets. The shaft turns the cross-arm rack; casters at the arm ends roll on the tabletop and carry the load, so each tray passes through the sunniest side in turn.

04

Protect the produce

Inside the closed dome the produce is protected from dust, insects, birds and sudden rain, which open-sun drying cannot offer.

Key quantity: moisture content

Drying performance is judged by how fast the produce loses water. Moisture content on a wet basis is

Mwb = (Wi − Wd) / Wi × 100 %

where Wi is the initial mass of the sample and Wd its mass after drying. We will weigh samples at fixed intervals in the dryer and in open sun side by side.

      Laser cutting

      Flat sheet parts were cut on the Makerspace CO2 laser cutter. Laser cutting follows the 2D outline from the CAD file exactly, so parts come out accurate, repeatable and with clean edges, without any cutting tool touching the sheet.

      Workflow

      1. Draw the part outline in CAD and export it as a 2D vector file (DXF).
      2. Import the file into the laser software; set cut lines, power, speed and passes for the material.
      3. Place the sheet flat on the bed and set the laser focus height.
      4. Switch on the fume extraction, close the lid and start the job.
      5. Watch the cut through the windows; never leave the machine unattended.
      6. Wait for the fumes to clear, open the lid and remove the parts.

      Laser safety we followed

      • Dress smart, stay safe — tie back long hair and do not wear loose-fitting clothes (Makerspace safety poster).
      • Follow the Makerspace "7 ways to work safe with lasers" rules displayed next to the machine.
      • Keep the lid closed while the laser is firing; view the cut only through the viewing windows.
      • Run the fume extraction during cutting; wood smoke must not enter the room.
      • Never leave the machine unattended — wood can catch fire; know where the emergency stop and fire extinguisher are.
      • Cut only approved materials; never cut PVC or unknown plastics, which release toxic gases.

      Testing plan

      No test has been run yet. This is what we will measure, side by side with an open-sun control sample of the same produce, from about 9:00 to 17:00 on clear days.

      Moisture loss

      Weigh tray samples every hour on a digital balance; calculate moisture content and drying time to the target moisture.

      Pending

      Temperature

      Air temperature inside the dome at each tray level vs ambient, with a digital thermometer or thermocouples.

      Pending

      Relative humidity

      RH inside and outside the dome with a hygrometer — warm, dry air is what removes moisture.

      Pending

      Solar radiation

      Record sunlight intensity (W/m²) with a solar power meter so tests on different days can be compared.

      Pending

      Rotation

      Rack speed in rpm, smooth running of casters and bearing, and the motor's current draw and power.

      Pending

      Uniformity

      Compare moisture loss of trays on different levels and positions, with and without rotation.

      SEC 09

      Safety and maintenance

      • Keep hands clear of the sprocket and chain drive while the motor is powered; fit a guard over the drive.
      • Secure all motor wiring and keep it away from the moving rack and from water.
      • Check casters, bearing and welds before each run; the rack must turn without rubbing the dome.
      • Galvanised trays: use food-grade liners or keep produce from direct contact if the coating is damaged.
      • Clean trays and the inside of the dome after each batch to prevent mould and insects.
      • Acrylic dome: clean with mild soap and a soft cloth only; avoid solvents that crack or cloud it.
